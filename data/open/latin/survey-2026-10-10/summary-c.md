## Which lemmatiser to evaluate first, and why

1. **LatinCy `la_core_web_lg` 3.9.8** (MIT weights, 297 MB wheel from Hugging Face, CPU). It is the only candidate that is a packaged, maintained, permissively licensed pipeline with a lemmatiser trained on verse (LASLA Catullus and Horace are in its training data, which also means LASLA cannot serve as its held-out test — see below). Claimed LEMMA_ACC 95.26 (README) / 94.15 (model index). Run it twice in the 300-token test: as shipped, and with `lookup_lemmatizer` disabled, because the lookup table package `la-latincy-lookups` is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; if the lookup-free run holds up, the NC table never touches Melos data. Known tokenizer facts to control for: only `-que` is split (not `-ne`, `-ue`), macrons are stripped, `norm_` is u/i-folded while the lemma spelling convention is mixed (LASLA u-only vs UD v) — the index must fold u/v and i/j before matching headwords.
2. **`la_stanza_latincy`** (MIT tag, claims 97.87 lemma accuracy on UD + LASLA) and **Stanza `stanza-la`** (Apache-2.0 card; Perseus lemma 83.57) as the second and third runs — both are Stanza, so one harness covers them; they are also the candidates for the contextual rescoring role OdyCy plays in the Greek index.
3. **Morpheus Latin** (CC BY-SA 3.0 US) as the *parser* in the Greek sense: it returns every analysis with Lewis & Short lemma keys, which is what `build_lemma_index.py` expects from Morpheus today; it needs a new container built from `stemlib/Latin` (the current image is Greek-only). **Whitaker's WORDS** (permissive) is the fallback analyser for forms Morpheus misses.
4. Not for production: UDPipe models and LatinPipe's model (CC BY-NC-SA), LatMor (NC + paid), LEMLAT 3 (NC); Collatinus only as a separate GPL process (its lexicon is GPL v2+, so not a bundled headword list). The CLTK backoff lemmatiser is legacy (CLTK 2.x removed it) and has no accuracy claim.

## Which dictionary gives the composer's glosses legally

- **Lewis & Short** (PerseusDL/lexica, CC BY-SA 4.0 with the Perseus credit line; 77.4 MB TEI; headwords carry macrons and breves) for full senses, and **Lewis, Elementary Latin Dictionary** (same repo and licence, 14.5 MB) for short pop-up glosses. Both must be fetched on the laptop or streamed (over this survey's 50 MB cap; the box is at 98 % disk).
- **DCC Latin Core Vocabulary** (CC BY-SA 3.0, ~1,000 headwords with one-line definitions and frequency ranks) for the commonest words.
- **Wiktionary via Kaikki** (CC BY-SA + GFDL, 1.2 GB JSONL) for inflection tables, principal parts and macronised headwords rather than glosses.
- **Latin WordNet 2.0** (CC BY-SA 4.0, 74,795 lemmas with prosody and principal parts) for synonym lists and LiLa alignment.
- Not usable as things stand: **Gaffiot 2016** (licence unverified: no statement found on gaffiot.org, gaffiot.fr or the reachable Gréco page; ask the author), **Logeion** (off limits).

## Open lemmatised Catullus/Horace data for the held-out check and as recorded lemmas

- **LASLA on GitHub (CIRCSE/LASLA v1.0.1)**, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: human-verified lemma + POS + morphology for *all* of Catullus (13,129 words, 3,161 lemma strings) and *all* of Horace (45,007 words: Odes I–IV, Epodes, Carmen Saeculare, Ars Poetica, Epistles, Satires), each token linked to a LiLa lemma id. This is the held-out gold for every candidate except LatinCy and `la_stanza_latincy`, which were trained on it (score those on the 300 hand-checked tokens only, or on a LASLA slice after confirming it is outside their training split). As *recorded lemmas* in the product index its NC/SA terms need an owner decision or a request to LASLA/CIRCSE; the raw files (17.4 MB, hashed) are in the scratchpad for the evaluation.
- **EvaLatin 2020 Horace Odes gold** (13,290 tokens, lemma + UPOS; CC BY-NC-SA 4.0): the same LASLA annotation packaged as a published test set, handy for comparing against reported system scores.
- **UD_Latin-CIRCSE** (CC BY-SA 4.0): Seneca's tragedies and Tacitus' Germania with LASLA-derived lemmas — the only BY-SA verse treebank; use it to check lemma-spelling conventions and as recorded lemmas for the Seneca choral lyrics in the minor-poets list.
- **UD_Latin-Perseus** (NC 2.5; Vergil, Ovid, Propertius; no Catullus/Horace) and **Tesserae** (texts of both poets but no lemma annotations and no licence file) do not add gold Catullus/Horace tokens. No lemmatised Catullus/Horace was found on Hugging Face.
- **LiLa Lemma Bank** (CC BY-SA 4.0; 168,789 lemmas; SPARQL endpoint and 11 MB SQL dump) is the headword id space to record against, since LASLA, Latin WordNet and Lewis & Short entries are all linked to it.

- Stanza `stanza-la` and the LatinCy retrains carry Apache/MIT tags on weights trained on NC treebanks; Melos should decide once whether a model trained on NC data may be run for a commercial site (the Greek survey's rule dropped NC *texts*; it said nothing about models).
- Tesserae, Gaffiot 2016, pie-extended's `lasla` model and LatinISE have no verifiable licence: ask the authors or leave out.
