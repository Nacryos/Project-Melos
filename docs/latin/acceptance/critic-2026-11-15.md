## The poem as a whole

gives p90 57.1, so at the top of his range); the turn is the an of line 4; the close answers tacitus with

## What the tools got wrong or could not tell me

1. **bella read as bellum (war).** L6 ("bella at syllable 3 (bellum NOUN)"), L5 ("bellum + uenustus: no
   parallel") and L14 ("lemma=bellum, earliest Prisc.") all judged the wrong word. The real evidence for the
   line (bellus at 24.7, "Non est homo bellus?") never appeared; I found it by a hand query.
2. **L3 agreement is blind to the one real agreement question.** First run: PASS, flagging the noun risus and
   tacitus as "agrees with no noun" and saying nothing of bella / venusta (feminine singular, referent amores,
   masculine plural). A second run minutes later (on the repaired text) added "line 3: passes only if venusta
   ...": the check changed under me while other agents edited it, so the saved report and the final report
   disagree.
3. **L5 collocation is noise at this length.** Most "no parallel" rows pair a word with the proper name or with a
   word of the next line (fabullus + celo, celo + satis); the Horace "parallels" are whole-passage word bags (the
   example for risus + dico is an alphabetical word list of an epistle), not constructions. It found none of the
   loci that decided the verdicts (24.7, 16.4, 29.16, 39.6, 40.1, 7.7, Hor. C. 1.27.17).
4. **Nothing checks particle order or the force of a question.** modo before its imperative, non est ...?
   expecting "yes", satis after its adjective: all passed L10. These took the dictionary and the grammar by hand.
5. **L6's register warning cannot tell chance from register.** celo "not in the polymetrics" was answered only by
   Lewis & Short's author counts (Ter., Plaut., Cic.), which L6 does not consult; meanwhile age (only in 61 and
   63, never in the hendecasyllables) was counted as a polymetric lemma and not flagged.
6. **L13 says PASS over two rarities in the closing line** (trochee base 6.0 %, monosyllabic close 1.3 %), and
   its count (7 of 550 lines) disagrees with rubric section 7 (17 lines, 3 %).
7. **The corpus is Catullus and Horace only** (115 and 121 passages); the brief promises Virgil and "the other
   poets". Horace's Satires did not come back in any query (L&S's Hor. S. 2,3,276 could not be read). Every
   Virgil citation above is second-hand through Lewis & Short.
8. **No command line for the concordance or the lemma index.** The brief lists them as agent tools only; I wrote
   a helper against the sqlite store. A query for "verum" returns nothing without the u/v normalisation, with no
   message.
9. **Dictionary lookups by form land on the wrong or an unwieldy entry.** `la_dict.py tacitus` opens on taceo;

## Coordinator's addendum: line 5 as kept

The critic's repair (*quidquid est, age dic*) repeats *age* from line 1 and left the choice open. The line kept is

```
quid sit, dic modo: nam tacebimus nos.
```

- *quid sit* + *dic*: indirect question after *dic*, as Cat. 55.15 *dic nobis ubi sis futurus*.
- Spondee base (86.7 % of Catullus's lines) instead of the trochee of the first draft (6.0 %).
- Lost: the echo of Cat. 6.15 *quidquid habes boni malique*. Still rare: the monosyllabic close *nos*.
